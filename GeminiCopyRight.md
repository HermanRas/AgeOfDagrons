# Google Gemini output — the terms as they stand

**Transposed 2026-09-04.** This file exists because AOD ships AI-generated art — the
launcher icon, the boot splash, the whole HUD icon set and the six HOW TO PLAY pages, all
generated by the project owner with Google Gemini (see
[`game/assets/LICENCES.md`](game/assets/LICENCES.md)) — and "Google doesn't claim it" was
being asserted in three files with **no link to the document that says so**. An
attribution claim with no citation is the same class of problem as an undeclared asset.

⚠️ **THESE TERMS CARRY DATES AND THE DATES MOVE.** Every quote below is dated from the page
it was read off. Google folded the one document everybody links to into a different one in
2024 (see §1.1), so **re-read before relying on this for anything that matters** and update
this file rather than a second copy of it.

---

## 1. Which document governs, and it depends on the tier

There is no single "Google AI output copyright" page. Four different agreements answer the
question, and **which one applies is decided by how the output was generated, not by who
generated it.**

| Tier | The operative document | Read on | Applies to AOD? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Consumer Gemini** (free, or a paid Google AI Pro / Ultra subscription) | [Google Terms of Service](https://policies.google.com/terms) — §*Your content* → *License* | effective **30 July 2026** | ✅ **YES — this is ours** |
| **Gemini API / AI Studio** (developer) | [Gemini API Additional Terms of Service](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) | effective **23 March 2026**, last updated **28 April 2026** | no |
| **Gemini for Google Workspace** (work/school account) | [Google Workspace Service Specific Terms](https://workspace.google.com/terms/service-terms/) + [Generative AI in Workspace Privacy Hub](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/generative-ai/generative-ai-in-google-workspace-privacy-hub) | current | no |
| **Vertex AI** (Google Cloud, enterprise) | [Google Cloud Service Specific Terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms) + [Generative AI Indemnified Services](https://cloud.google.com/terms/generative-ai-indemnified-services) | current | no |

Two policies sit across all four tiers:

- [Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy](https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy)
  — last modified **17 December 2024**.
- The main ToS's *Don't abuse our services* section, quoted in §2.2 below.

### 1.1 ⚠️ The link that looks right and is retired

<https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai> — **"Generative AI Additional Terms of
Service", last modified 9 August 2023.** This is the URL almost every article and forum
answer cites, and the page itself says the terms **no longer apply as of 22 May 2024**,
when Google moved the AI provisions into the main Terms of Service. It is still served, it
still reads like a live agreement, and it is not one.

**Cite the main ToS for the consumer tier.** This row is here so the retired link does not
get pasted back in by somebody doing the same search we did.

## 2. The consumer tier, verbatim — the one that governs AOD's art

From the [Google Terms of Service](https://policies.google.com/terms), effective
**30 July 2026**.

### 2.1 Ownership — Google does not claim it

Under *Your content* → *License*:

> Some of our services allow you to generate original content. Google won't claim ownership
> over that content.

and, on content generally:

> Your content remains yours, which means that you retain any intellectual property rights
> that you have in your content.

Note the exact shape of the second sentence, because it is doing less than a skim suggests:
it preserves **whatever rights you have**. It does not grant any. Whether rights exist at
all is §3's question, and the answer there is not "yes".

The developer tier states the same thing with one clause the consumer ToS leaves implicit,
and it is worth reading even though it does not bind us — from the
[Gemini API Additional Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms):

> Google won't claim ownership over that content. You acknowledge that Google may generate
> the same or similar content for others and that we reserve all rights to do so.

**"Not owned by Google" is not the same as "exclusively yours."** The model may hand a
near-identical icon to the next person who asks. That is the honest reading of what we
have, and it is compatible with shipping — it is not compatible with treating the icon set
as a project asset we could stop somebody else using.

### 2.2 What is prohibited

Two restrictions in *Don't abuse our services* touch generated output directly:

- **No passing it off as human-made** — the ToS prohibits "misleading others into thinking
  that generative AI content was created by a human." **AOD complies by construction:**
  `game/assets/LICENCES.md` declares every generated file as generated, `CREDITS.md` says
  so on the Credits screen, and this file exists.
- **No training on it** — it prohibits using "AI-generated content from our services to
  develop machine learning models." Not something this project does.

Gemini output also carries Google's **SynthID** watermark, so the files are detectable as
AI-generated whatever any of our documents claim. Another reason the declaration is worth
making honestly: it cannot be quietly wrong.

### 2.3 Commercial use

Permitted, on every tier, with no royalty and no revenue share. Nothing in the consumer
ToS conditions commercial use of generated output, and the API terms are explicitly aimed
at "professional or business purposes."

**AOD is free and open-source, so this is the least load-bearing fact here** — but it is
the one people ask about first, and a future paid release would not change the answer.

## 3. Copyright registration — the part that is NOT settled in our favour

Ownership and copyright are two different questions, and Google's terms only answer the
first. Whether the output attracts copyright *at all* is US law's answer, not Google's,
and it is **no**.

From the [U.S. Copyright Office's AI page](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/):

- **[Copyright Registration Guidance for Works Containing AI-Generated Material](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/ai_policy_guidance.pdf)**
  — 16 March 2023, **88 Fed. Reg. 16,190**. Purely AI-generated material is not
  registrable; copyright requires a human author.
- **[Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)**
  — 29 January 2025. Human contribution to a work containing AI-generated material can be
  registrable; the AI-generated portions are not.
- ***Thaler v. Perlmutter*** — the Court of Appeals affirmed the Office's refusal to
  register a purely machine-generated work.

**What this means for AOD's icons, stated plainly:** they are ours to ship, ours to
modify, and **probably not ours to restrict.** Treat them as effectively unprotectable
rather than as MIT-licensed project code. That is a real difference and it cuts exactly one
way — it costs us nothing while we are giving the game away, and it means we could not
object if someone lifted the HUD set. If that ever needs to be false, the fix is drawn
originals, not a licence header.

The HOW TO PLAY pages are the interesting case and the answer is already recorded: they are
screen captures of this game with instructions painted over them, so the layer *under* the
annotation is our own rendering of 0 A.D. art and **carries CC-BY-SA 3.0 regardless.** The
AI question applies to the annotation, not to the page.

## 4. Training on our inputs — a privacy fact, not a licence fact

This does not affect our right to ship anything. It is recorded because *how* an asset was
made is part of its provenance, and because the tier table is the thing people get wrong.

From the [Gemini Apps Privacy Hub](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961),
privacy questions updated **10 August 2026**:

> Google uses your activity to provide, develop, and improve its services (including
> training generative AI models).

With **Keep Activity** off:

> Your future chats won't appear in your Activity, and won't be used to train our AI
> models, unless you choose to send Google feedback.

and, either way:

> Even if your Keep Activity setting is off or you use temporary chats, Google still uses
> your chats to respond to you and help protect Google, our users, and the public,
> including with help from human reviewers.

⚠️ **A PAID CONSUMER SUBSCRIPTION IS NOT AN OPT-OUT, AND THIS IS THE POINT MOST OFTEN GOT
WRONG.** "Paid means no training" is true of the **Gemini API's** paid tier — which states
"Google doesn't use your prompts … or responses to improve our products" — and of
**Workspace**, whose Privacy Hub commits that "your content is not human reviewed or used
for Generative AI model training outside your domain without permission." **It is not a
documented property of a paid Google AI Pro / Ultra consumer subscription**, which is the
account AOD's art was generated on. There, the control is the **Keep Activity** toggle at
<https://myactivity.google.com/product/gemini>, and it is on by default.

Our files say "paid account" because that is the factual provenance. **They should not be
read as claiming the prompts were withheld from training** — nothing in this project ever
established that, and it would need the toggle checked, not the receipt.

| Tier | Inputs used to train, by default |
|---|---|
| Consumer Gemini, free **or paid** | **yes** — governed by Keep Activity, on by default |
| Gemini API / AI Studio, unpaid quota | yes |
| Gemini API, paid | no |
| Gemini for Workspace | no, not outside your domain without permission |
| Vertex AI | no |

## 5. Indemnification — enterprise only, and we do not have it

Google Cloud offers a generative-AI IP indemnity covering both the training data behind its
models **and the generated output**, listed in
[Generative AI Indemnified Services](https://cloud.google.com/terms/generative-ai-indemnified-services)
— Vertex AI with GA foundation models, Gemini for Google Cloud, Gemini Enterprise and
others. It carries real limits, including exclusions on specific outputs and on the
customer's handling of the safety filters.

**AOD has none of this.** It attaches to a Google Cloud agreement, not to a consumer
subscription. Recorded so nobody reaches for "Google indemnifies us" — the correct position
is §3's: the icons are ours to ship and probably unprotectable, and the exposure that
matters is a third-party claim we would carry ourselves.
