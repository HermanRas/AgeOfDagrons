Age of Dragons · player identity

Player Colour Ladder

Colour is the only thing that distinguishes one player from another in v1 — no faction, no unique unit, no silhouette difference. That makes these eight values a legibility requirement, not a styling preference. Eight hues cannot all be separated by hue alone, so four of them are separated by lightness instead.

01

The roster

Slot order is part of the design. Most matches are 1v1 or 2v2, so slots 1–4 are the four most separable colours; green sits at slot 5 so it never meets yellow before a five-player game. L* is CIE lightness — 0 is black, 100 is white.

02

Why the ladder exists

Red–green colour blindness collapses red, orange, green and yellow onto one axis. Those four therefore have to differ in brightness, spread about 16 L* apart. The blue–yellow axis survives every form of colour blindness, so colours in different lanes are free to sit close in lightness — and the pairs that do are always cross-lane.

06

What this still depends on

The shader's blend mode decides whether any of this survives. 0 A.D. multiplies the player colour into the texture, and isobake reproduces that at bake time. Under pure multiply, white is a no-op — that player simply looks untinted — and dark colours crush the texture detail underneath, compressing this whole ladder back together.

  • Mix toward the colour, or take its hue and keep the texture's own luminance. Either preserves the spread; multiply does not.
  • Gaia takes no tint. Some 0 A.D. wildlife actors declare a player-colour mask anyway — the sheep does — so the shader must key off who owns the entity, not off whether the sprite has a mask.
  • The outline comes free. A team-colour outline is a second pass over the same mask, and it removes what little risk is left, because an outline reads as shape rather than hue.
